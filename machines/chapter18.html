2.1

Induction Motor as a Rotating Transformer

The three-phase induction motor operates on the same fundamental principle as a transformer — electromagnetic induction across an air gap — making it aptly described as a rotating transformer. The stator acts as the primary winding and the rotor (always short-circuited or loaded mechanically) acts as the secondary.[1]

When rated voltage is applied to the stator, a rotating magnetic field (RMF) is set up at synchronous speed \(N_s\). This RMF links the rotor winding, inducing an EMF \(SE_2\) at slip frequency \(sf\). The rotor current \(I_2'\) lags the induced EMF by the rotor impedance angle.

💡
Key On-Load Behaviour

On load, rotor current increases proportionally to produce its own flux in the air gap which opposes the stator RMF (Lenz's law). Therefore the stator draws more current proportionally to maintain the air-gap flux constant. Whenever the rotor is loaded, it will immediately call current from the stator \((kI_2')\) — with load the power factor improves but operates at low PF compared to an equivalent transformer.

Fig 2.1 — Phasor diagram of induction motor under load. The total stator current I₁ = I₀ + kI₂'. With load PF improves but remains lagging.
Basis: Nagrath & Kothari [1], §6.3; Chapman [2], §6.3

Transformer vs Induction Motor — Detailed Comparison

Understanding the similarities and key differences between a transformer and an induction motor is essential for the equivalent circuit development and is a frequent GATE/IES topic.[1][2]

🔑
Why IM is Called a Rotating Transformer

Both stator RMF and rotor RMF run at synchronous speed in the air gap, making the rotor rotate at any speed N. The absolute flux in space always rotates at synchronous speed wrt a stationary point, but wrt the rotor it is at \(sN_s\). The TF equivalent circuit is used for representing IM for analysis purposes. However there are 2 major differences: (i) frequency of rotor is slip frequency, (ii) loading is mechanical across the shaft.

2.3

Exact Equivalent Circuit (Referred to Stator)

The exact equivalent circuit of the three-phase induction motor is developed exactly like the transformer equivalent circuit, with one important modification to account for mechanical power output and rotor slip frequency.[1]

Fig 2.2 — Exact equivalent circuit of 3-phase induction motor referred to stator. Rₗ' = R₂'(1/s − 1) represents the mechanical power output per phase.
Basis: Nagrath & Kothari [1], §6.4, Fig 6.9

Power Flow in the Equivalent Circuit

The equivalent circuit allows us to trace the power flow from input to output. Starting from the supply voltage \(V_1\), the power flows as follows:

Power balance per phase \[ P_{in} = P_{stator\,cu} + P_{stator\,core} + P_{rotor\,cu} + P_{mech\,output} \] \[ P_{air\,gap} = P_{rotor\,cu} + P_{mech} = 3I_2'^2 \frac{R_2'}{s} \] \[ P_{rotor\,cu} = s \cdot P_{air\,gap} \qquad P_{mech} = (1-s) \cdot P_{air\,gap} \]
No Load Condition (s → 0)
  • →\(R_L' \rightarrow \infty\) (Open circuit like a transformer)
  • →Rotor draws no current
  • →Motor runs at synchronous speed \(N_s\)
  • →All air gap power goes to mechanical output
Full Load (Rated) Condition (s → 1)
  • →\(R_L' \rightarrow 0\) (Short circuit like a transformer)
  • →Rotor is blocked, \(N = 0\)
  • →All air gap power dissipated as rotor Cu loss
  • →Equivalent to blocked rotor test condition
2.4

Rotor Frequency, Slip & Equivalent Rotor Circuit

This is the most critical conceptual section in IM theory. The two major differences between a transformer and an IM that must be resolved for proper equivalent circuit development are: (i) frequency of rotor is slip frequency, and (ii) loading is mechanical across the shaft.[1]

Slip Definition

Slip and rotor quantities \[ s = \frac{N_s - N}{N_s} \qquad \text{(dimensionless, 0 to 1)} \] \[ E_{rotor} = sE_2 \quad \text{(at standstill } E_{rotor} = E_2\text{)} \] \[ f_{rotor} = sf \quad \text{(rotor frequency = slip × supply frequency)} \] \[ I_2' = \frac{sE_2}{R_2 + jsX_2} = \frac{E_2}{\frac{R_2}{s} + jX_2} \]
Fig 2.3 — Rotor circuit transformation. Dividing by 's' brings rotor to stator frequency. Splitting R₂/s reveals the physical separation between copper loss and mechanical output.
Basis: Nagrath & Kothari [1], §6.4; Chapman [2], §6.4
⚠️
Critical GATE Concept — Slip Range

At no-load: \(s \approx 0\), \(R_L \rightarrow \infty\) (open circuit). At standstill/blocked rotor: \(s = 1\), \(R_L = 0\) (short circuit). At rated full load, typical slip is 2–8% for squirrel cage motors. Rotor copper loss = s × Air gap power — this is a favourite GATE numerical formula.

2.5

Referred Equivalent Circuit & Approximate Circuit

Referring the rotor quantities to the stator side using the effective turns ratio \(k = N_2/N_1 = E_2/E_1\) (at standstill), we get the complete referred equivalent circuit. The transformation equations for rotor resistance and reactance referred to stator are:[1][2]

Referred quantities (rotor → stator) \[ k = \frac{N_2}{N_1} = \frac{E_2 \text{ at standstill}}{E_1} \] \[ R_2' = \left(\frac{N_1}{N_2}\right)^2 R_2 = \frac{R_2}{k^2} \qquad X_2' = \frac{X_2}{k^2} \] \[ R_L' = \frac{R_2}{k^2}\left(\frac{1}{s} - 1\right) = \frac{R_2'}{k^2}\left(\frac{1-s}{s}\right) \] \[ I_2' = k I_2 \quad \text{(referred rotor current)} \]
Fig 2.4 — Approximate equivalent circuit with shunt branch moved to input terminals. R₀₁ = R₁ + R₂', X₀₁ = X₁ + X₂'. Used for simplified calculations.
Basis: Nagrath & Kothari [1], §6.5; IEEE Standard 112
✅
IEEE Standard Representation

In the IEEE standard circuit, the shunt branch resistance Rc is eliminated from the standard circuit but core losses and mechanical losses should be included in the net power calculation. This gives more accurate analysis. The approximate equivalent circuit further simplifies by neglecting the shunt branch entirely (suitable for squirrel cage motors where \(X_m\) is large).

2.7

No-Load Test — Determination of Shunt Branch Parameters

The No-Load test (NL test) is performed to determine the magnetising reactance \(X_m\) and the core loss resistance \(R_c\). It is analogous to the Open-Circuit (OC) test of a transformer.[1]

Fig 2.5 — No-load test circuit. LPF wattmeters essential. Rated voltage applied, motor runs freely with no mechanical load. Rotor core loss & rotor Cu loss neglected.
Basis: Nagrath & Kothari [1], §6.8

No-Load Test Procedure & Calculations

Apply rated voltage to the stator and allow the motor to run freely on no-load. Connect necessary meters and essentially LPF wattmeters as the no-load power factor is very low (typically 0.1–0.2). The 3-phase power is measured using the 2-wattmeter method.

No-load test equations \[ \omega_{NL} = \omega_1 + \omega_2 \quad \text{(algebraic sum — 2 wattmeter)} \] \[ \omega_{NL} = 3I_{NL}^2 R_1 + \text{Constant loss (rotational)} \] \[ Z_{NL} = \frac{V_{NL}}{I_{NL}} \qquad Z_{NL} = R_1 + j(X_m + X_1) \] \[ X_m = \sqrt{Z_{NL}^2 - (X_m + X_1)^2} - X_1 \] \[ \phi_{NL} = \cos^{-1}\left(\frac{\omega_{NL}}{\sqrt{3} V_{NL} I_{NL}}\right) \]
📐
Total Loss Breakdown at No-Load

At no-load, output power = 0. So the wattmeter reading represents total losses: Stator Cu loss (small, since \(I_{NL}\) is small) + Stator core loss + Mechanical loss (friction + windage). Rotor core loss is negligible (due to low slip frequency) and rotor Cu loss is negligible (slip ≈ 0). To separate iron and mech losses, the DC test gives stator resistance to eliminate stator Cu loss: Rotational loss = ω_NL − 3I²_NL R₁

2.8

Blocked Rotor Test — Leakage Impedance Determination

The Blocked Rotor (BR) test (analogous to Short Circuit test of transformer) determines the series leakage impedance: \(R_{BR}\), \(X_{BR}\), and hence \(R_2'\), \(X_1'\), \(X_2'\). The rotor is mechanically blocked to prevent rotation (\(N = 0\), \(s = 1\)).[1]

A reduced voltage is carefully applied using a suitable auto-transformer (typically 5–10% of rated voltage) to circulate rated current. The machine must not run during this test.

Blocked rotor test equations \[ Z_{BR} = \frac{V_{BR}}{I_{BR}} = R_{BR} + jX_{BR} \] \[ \omega_{BR} = \sqrt{3}\,V_{BR}\,I_{BR}\cos\phi_{BR} \] \[ \phi_{BR} = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\omega_{BR}}{\sqrt{3}\,V_{BR}\,I_{BR}}\right) \] \[ X_{BR} \approx X_1 + X_2' \quad\Rightarrow\quad X_1 \approx X_2' \approx \frac{X_{BR}}{2} \] \[ R_{BR} = R_1 + \frac{R_2 X_m^2}{R_2^2 + (X_2 + X_m)^2} \approx R_1 + R_2'\bigg(\frac{X_m}{X_m + X_2'}\bigg)^2 \] \[ R_2' = (R_{BR} - R_1)\left(\frac{X_{2m}}{X_m}\right)^2 \]
Fig 2.6 — Blocked rotor test equivalent circuit with s = 1 (RL = 0). Wattmeter readings give stator Cu loss + rotor Cu loss at rated current. Mech loss = 0 (motor not running).
Basis: Nagrath & Kothari [1], §6.8; Fitzgerald [3], §6.5
⚠️
There is NO Practical Method to Separate X₁ and X₂'

From the blocked rotor test, \(X_{BR} = X_1 + X_2'\) is obtained. However, there is no practical method to separately measure \(X_1\) and \(X_2'\). Therefore, it is approximated as: \(X_1 \cong X_2' \cong X_{BR}/2\). This is a standard assumption used in all calculations. X₂ → X₂' because referred to stator.

Converting BR Test Values to Rated Voltage Base

The blocked rotor test is performed at reduced voltage (5–10% rated). For the Circle Diagram, these values must be adjusted to rated voltage base:

Blocked rotor to rated voltage base \[ I_{SN} = \frac{V_{rated}}{V_{BR}} \times I_{BR} \] \[ \omega_{SN} = \left(\frac{I_{SN}}{I_{BR}}\right)^2 \omega_{BR} \] \[ \phi_{BR} = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\omega_{BR}}{\sqrt{3}\,V_{BR}\,I_{BR}}\right) \]
2.9

DC Test & Separation of Rotational Losses

The DC test (or Voltmeter-Ammeter method) determines the DC resistance of the stator winding. This is necessary to find the AC resistance \(R_{1AC}\) which accounts for skin effect and temperature rise.[1]

DC test to AC resistance conversion \[ R_{1DC} = \frac{V_{DC}}{I_{DC}} \quad \text{(per phase, from V/A ratio)} \] \[ R_{1AC} = (1.2 \text{ to } 1.6) \times R_{1DC} \quad \text{(accounts for skin effect + hot resistance)} \] \[ \text{Rotational loss} = \omega_{NL} - 3 I_{NL}^2 R_1 \]

Separation of Iron Loss and Mechanical Loss

A special No-Load test is performed to separate iron losses from mechanical (friction + windage) losses. Different voltages (40% to 125% rated) are applied and the rotational loss is plotted as a function of voltage squared. Since iron losses ∝ φ ∝ V² and mechanical losses ∝ N (approximately constant speed on NL), extrapolation to V = 0 gives the mechanical loss intercept.[1]

Fig 2.7 — Separation of rotational losses by extrapolation. Intercept at V = 0 gives mechanical (friction + windage) loss. Difference at any voltage gives iron loss at that voltage.
Basis: Nagrath & Kothari [1], §6.8; Fitzgerald [3], §6.5
2.10

Circle Diagram — Graphical Analysis Tool

The Circle Diagram is the graphical representation of the approximate equivalent circuit of an induction motor. It provides a rapid visual method to determine all performance parameters — efficiency, slip, torque, output power — at any load condition without solving complex circuit equations.[1][3]

🔑
Why the Locus is a Circle

The locus of current in a series R-L circuit is a circle when the voltage \(U\) and frequency are fixed and only the resistance varies. The IM current locus with varying load (varying slip → varying \(R_L'\)) traces a semicircle. A chord in a circle is bisected if and only if it passes through its centre. The circle diagram is plotted on a vertical voltage axis (V-axis as reference).

Fig 2.8 — Circle Diagram of IM. Point P on the circle represents any operating load. Vertical projections from P give all performance quantities. AB = output line, AK = torque line.
Basis: Nagrath & Kothari [1], §6.9; Sen [4], §4.8

Steps to Draw the Circle Diagram

Construction Steps
  • 1.Draw no-load current I₀ with PF angle φ_NL (wrt V-axis)
  • 2.Draw ISN with PF angle φ_BR
  • 3.Bisect chord I₀-ISN; perpendicular bisector meets horizontal line AE at center C
  • 4.Draw circle with centre C, radius CA
  • 5.Join AK (torque line) where K is located on horizontal from stator Cu loss scale
Key Quantities from Circle
  • →Max torque: Tangent to circle parallel to torque line → point X
  • →Max output: Tangent parallel to output line → point Y
  • →Starting torque: Length BK × power scale / (2πNs/60)
  • →Slip: s = RQ/RP
  • →Efficiency: η = PQ/PT
Circle diagram power scale and torque formulas \[ \ell(BG)\text{ cm} = \omega_{SN} \text{ (watts)} \Rightarrow 1\text{cm} = \frac{\omega_{SN}}{\ell(BG)}\text{ W/cm} \] \[ T_{st} \propto \text{Rotor Cu loss at } s=1 = \ell(BK) \times \text{power scale} \] \[ T_{st} = \frac{60}{2\pi N_s} \times \ell(BK) \times \text{power scale} \quad \text{N-m} \] \[ T_g = \frac{60}{2\pi N_s} \times \ell(RP) \times \text{power scale} \quad \text{N-m} \] \[ s = \frac{RQ}{RP}, \quad N = N_s(1-s) \]

GATE & IES Previous Year Questions

GATE EE A 3-phase induction motor runs at almost synchronous speed at no-load and slows down to slip s = 0.05 at full load. The ratio of rotor copper loss to rotor input (air gap power) is: GATE 2018
  • (A) 0.10
  • (B) 0.05 ✓ (s × Pair gap = rotor Cu loss → ratio = s = 0.05)
  • (C) 0.95
  • (D) 0.50

Explanation: The fundamental relation: Rotor Cu loss = \(s \times P_{air\,gap}\). Therefore ratio = \(s = 0.05\). This is directly from the equivalent circuit: the load resistor \(R_L = R_2'(1-s)/s\) represents mech. power, and \(R_2'\) alone represents rotor Cu loss.

GATE EE In the equivalent circuit of an induction motor referred to stator, if R₂' = 1Ω and slip s = 0.05, the load resistance R_L' representing the mechanical power output is: GATE 2015
  • (A) 1 Ω
  • (B) 5 Ω
  • (C) 19 Ω ✓
  • (D) 20 Ω

Solution: \(R_L' = R_2'\left(\frac{1}{s} - 1\right) = 1\times\left(\frac{1}{0.05} - 1\right) = 1 \times 19 = 19\,\Omega\)

IES E&T The no-load test of a 3-phase induction motor is performed to determine which of the following parameters? IES 2016
  • (A) Rotor resistance R₂
  • (B) Magnetising reactance Xm and core loss resistance Rc ✓
  • (C) Leakage reactance X₁ + X₂'
  • (D) Stator resistance R₁

Explanation: The no-load test ≡ OC test of transformer → determines shunt branch parameters (Xm, Rc). The blocked rotor test ≡ SC test → determines series branch (R₁, R₂', X₁, X₂'). DC test gives R₁ separately.

GATE EE In the circle diagram of a 3-phase induction motor, the vertical distance BK (where B is the ISN point and K is on the torque line directly below B) represents: GATE 2012
  • (A) Stator copper loss at ISN
  • (B) Rotor copper loss at starting (s=1) ✓
  • (C) Maximum output power
  • (D) No-load loss

Explanation: At starting, \(s = 1\), all air gap power goes to rotor Cu loss (mech output = 0). BK represents rotor Cu loss at blocked rotor condition. Starting torque ∝ BK. This is why: \(T_{st} \propto \text{Rotor Cu loss at }s=1\).

IES E&T Compare a transformer and an induction motor: At standstill (s=1), which statement is INCORRECT? IES 2019
  • (A) Induced EMF in rotor = E₂ (full standstill EMF)
  • (B) Rotor frequency = stator supply frequency
  • (C) Mechanical output is maximum at standstill ✗ (Incorrect statement)
  • (D) The IM behaves like a short-circuited transformer

Explanation: At standstill s=1: EMF = E₂ (full), frequency = f (same as stator), the machine behaves like an SC transformer (RL = 0). But mechanical output is ZERO at standstill since N=0, P_mech = (1-s)×P_airgap = 0. Hence C is the incorrect statement.

Quick Revision — Key Formulas for GATE/IES

Slip

\(s = \frac{N_s - N}{N_s}\), rotor freq \(= sf\)

Power Relations

Rotor Cu loss \(= s \cdot P_{ag}\), Mech = \((1-s)P_{ag}\)

Load Resistance

\(R_L' = R_2'\!\left(\tfrac{1}{s}-1\right) = R_2'\!\tfrac{1-s}{s}\)

Tests Summary

NL test → Xm, Rc; BR test → R₂', X₁, X₂'; DC test → R₁

Turns Ratio k

\(k = N_2/N_1 = E_2/E_1\), \(R_2' = R_2/k^2\), \(X_2' = X_2/k^2\)

Efficiency

\(\eta = P_{out}/P_{in} = P_{out}/(P_{out}+P_{const}+P_{var})\)
